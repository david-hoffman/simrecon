**Wiener sweep on the noisy finite-bead and corrected-cell simulations.** A–E show fluorescence truth, the mean of the same 15 exposures, and three registered reconstructions at z = −0.750 µm. F–I subtract the matched truth from the mean and each reconstruction. Both color scales are signed, centered at zero and use inverse hyperbolic sine mapping. Badges count clipped samples in the displayed crop. SU denotes simulation source units. J shows the root-mean-square (RMS) error relative to truth within the conditioned cell low band. K shows the baseline-subtracted valley drop as a fraction of the weaker peak for bead pair P14. Both horizontal axes show the public Wiener parameter w, with adjacent values separated by a factor of √10. The filter adds w² to the denominator, so this contribution increases tenfold at each step. The 15% drop criterion is one part of the frozen pair rule. The rule also requires two eligible maxima of at least 0.005 SU, at least 0.10 µm apart, in ordered truth neighborhoods. P14 at each Wiener value was 0.002 (0.125 µm), 0.006325 (0.125 µm), 0.020 (0.125 µm), 0.063246 (unresolved), 0.200 (unresolved). A missing valley drop has no percentage and is marked ×. The dotted vertical line marks the 0.020 reference. Each specimen has one noisy realization.
