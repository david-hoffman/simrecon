# Plan: make the generic delivery specification more efficient

**Version 1.0. Proposed October 3, 2026.**

Status: proposal only. Writing this plan does not amend the specification or authorize its adoption. The current [delivery specification](../agentic-software-delivery-v1.0/DELIVERY-SYSTEM-SPEC.md) remains authoritative. This plan concerns the reusable system; the [SIMrecon plan](agentic-delivery-repo-efficiency-plan.md) concerns its installation.

The proposed change is to scale session overhead to risk, preserve accepted evidence where it still applies, and make corrections smaller. Keep independent review where a wrong oracle or interpretation could validate the wrong product. Keep the 100% measured statement/branch coverage gate and full verification of the exact candidate. Section 5 separately proposes a conditional CI-authoritative alternative for eligible routine work; the current full-local-before-PR rule remains in force until an identified policy change is approved.

## Evidence and confidence

The current system requires four fresh root sessions per task, fresh A/B after test corrections, and a five-scenario default ([specification §§3–4](../agentic-software-delivery-v1.0/DELIVERY-SYSTEM-SPEC.md#3-step-0-clarify-and-approve-each-task)). It already permits narrow packets, unchanged-scenario redistribution, and applicable check reuse ([§9](../agentic-software-delivery-v1.0/DELIVERY-SYSTEM-SPEC.md#9-cost-and-completion)). Improve those rules before adding machinery.

The [SIMrecon plan](agentic-delivery-repo-efficiency-plan.md) records local observations: one completed conversion grew from 26 to 44 to 46 scenarios across three A/B review windows. Initial B found four oracle/test defects; later B caught a profile-dependent provenance oracle; D found an unjustified wavelength-key input restriction despite complete measured coverage. That supports preserving independent high-risk review. It does not establish an optimal slice size, model ranking, or measured savings. See the companion plan for token-accounting limits; historical checks were not rerun here.

The initial review included complete reads of all 14 files under root `LESSONS/`: the [format guide](../../LESSONS/README.md) and all 13 entries. The inventory found no nested lesson files or `docs/LESSONS/`. The [environment failure](../../LESSONS/20260930T191720Z-setup-coordinator-hidden-editable-install.md), [native parser failure](../../LESSONS/20260930T201435Z-setup-coordinator-native-boundary-compatibility.md), its [identity correction](../../LESSONS/20260930T201856Z-setup-coordinator-correct-native-cli-identity.md), [shared-oracle defect](../../LESSONS/20261002T173457Z-mrc-conversion-coordinator-profile-aware-provenance-oracle.md), [positive-input gap](../../LESSONS/20261002T175150Z-mrc-conversion-coordinator-positive-key-contract-gap.md), and [nested reviewer launch](../../LESSONS/20261002T182621Z-mrc-conversion-coordinator-fresh-role-launch-wording.md) describe different failure classes. Classify them before choosing a process change. Reading every lesson here was an explicitly requested plan-review exception; this follow-up reuses that reading. Routine workers still receive narrow permitted inputs.

The follow-up compares the owner's dphtools report with this proposal. Read-only [PR #16](https://github.com/david-hoffman/dphtools/pull/16) metadata confirms merge into `codex-main` as `4e00fe4` and 42 min 16 s from opening to merge. Its body reports 1,604 tests, complete statement/branch coverage, independent fallback review and no delivery-policy changes; those reports do not establish protected-branch enforcement. The 14 min 30 s local run, 89% release-test share and reviewer/environment costs remain reported cross-repository observations, not a verified SIMrecon baseline. Token savings remain unknown. The role tiers, contract reuse, narrow packets, concise state and evidence invalidation already overlap this plan; the additions below target prerequisite order, CI gate placement, deterministic waits, test hotspots and overlapping costs.

Confidence is high that narrower corrections and fewer repeated reads can reduce avoidable work. Confidence is moderate in the tier boundaries and low in any numerical savings estimate. A pilot that misses defects or shifts costs into owner review would change the recommendation.

## 1. Approve risk before selecting sessions

Amend the role table and opening paragraphs of [§4](../agentic-software-delivery-v1.0/DELIVERY-SYSTEM-SPEC.md#4-four-fresh-delivery-sessions), and the normal-path sentence in §9, to use this decision table. Record the selected tier and reason in the approved task.

| Proposed tier | Eligibility | Session route and acceptance |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanical or documentation | Non-normative prose, formatting, links, or deterministic edits with a reviewable diff; no changed executable behavior, dependency, verification rule, permission, or policy | One worker. Review the diff and relevant deterministic checks. Owner acceptance is sufficient; an independent reviewer is optional unless the approved contract requires one. |
| Bounded existing behavior or infrastructure maintenance | A small repair/refactor with established conventional expectations, or explicitly scoped dependency/lint/workflow maintenance preserving settled platform/check semantics; no new scientific meaning, binary representation, custom oracle, security/permission boundary, policy, or ambiguous interface | One worker may inspect implementation and author authorized tests and maintenance files. A fresh independent reviewer checks the oracle, behavior, infrastructure diff, preserved gates, scope, and exact passing candidate. The worker cannot approve their own repair. |
| High risk | Scientific/numerical behavior, binary formats, new custom oracles, security/permissions, substantial new interfaces, unresolved semantics, or uncertain impact | Retain fresh blind A test author, fresh blind B test reviewer, C implementer, and fresh D final reviewer. Preserve source-free A/B diagnostics and restricted-file rules. |

Risk determines the route, never changed-line count alone. Removing tests/assertions can erase accepted proof even with unchanged runtime; require a reviewed obligation mapping or promote uncertain impact. Uncertainty promotes a task. A mixed task takes the highest tier unless independently scoped contracts justify a split. A failed lower-tier check does not itself authorize a scientific or interface repair. Dependency, workflow and gate maintenance always requires independent review; changed execution authority/security boundaries use the high-risk route. Policy edits, including adoption of this plan, have a separate documentation path: explicit owner approval and independent policy review before activation. They cannot classify themselves as mechanical. Changed tests/fixtures always receive independent review; high-risk changes use A/B. Coverage, discovery, fixtures, and delivery-rule edits require their own authorized scope. Tiering never permits silently weakening checks.

Define ownership for the middle-tier worker/reviewer explicitly in the revised procedures; do not assign today's C packet and expect it to edit restricted tests or infrastructure. The high-risk route keeps C's restrictions. Documentation-only policy review does not require fabricated product-red evidence or scientific A/B tests.

Tradeoff: the middle tier loses blind test construction. Limit it to established expectations; require the reviewer to challenge the oracle against the approved contract. Promote it if that review exposes an uncertain expectation. The conversion observations support retaining the high-risk route.

## 2. Separate task approval from routine execution

Amend [§3](../agentic-software-delivery-v1.0/DELIVERY-SYSTEM-SPEC.md#3-step-0-clarify-and-approve-each-task) to say that explicit approval authorizes the named scope, tier, checks, budget, and routine role transitions. No repeat approval is needed to launch an already specified role, run authorized checks, fix an in-scope product defect within the remaining repair allowance, or make the approved unchanged-scenario redistribution.

For the first two tiers, a complete, explicit owner instruction may itself authorize the task. Record the actual instruction, scope, tier and inherited constraints; send a concise read-back without requiring a second “yes” merely because the record was written afterward. Missing material behavior or a new budget still needs an answer. High-risk contracts and changes to delivery policy retain explicit approval of the identified proposal. This is a proposed exception to the current universal read-back gate, not permission to infer approval from silence.

Renew approval for changed behavior, interfaces, permissions, data sharing, tier downgrade, budget extension, or exhausted repair allowance. Material unanswered questions still block dependent work. Architecture approval still does not approve a scientific feature. Silence remains no approval.

In the [task template](../agentic-software-delivery-v1.0/templates/TASK.md), add an “Authorized execution” line containing tier, role route, edit scope, checks, per-role model/effort selection, applicable local/CI gate mode, escalation triggers, and allowances. Update the [intake prompt](../agentic-software-delivery-v1.0/INTAKE-PROMPT.md) to ask for this once in the read-back.

## 3. Separate behavioral scenarios from coverage examples

Clarify the equivalent-data allowance already present in §3, its scenario-count paragraph, and [§5](../agentic-software-delivery-v1.0/DELIVERY-SYSTEM-SPEC.md#5-end-to-end-first-not-end-to-end-only): a scenario is a distinct approved decision and observable outcome. Multiple values that exercise that same decision may be coverage examples. A distinct rejection rule, interpretation, output guarantee, or boundary outcome is a separate scenario. Do not combine unrelated cases merely to evade the limit.

Keep five behavioral scenarios as an initial planning heuristic for distinct behavioral obligations. Approve a larger coherent contract once. Additional equivalent data or coverage examples within accepted behavior and budget do not trigger fresh approval. Add an optional coverage-example mapping under each scenario; B checks that the values introduce no unapproved meaning. Neither a branch count nor a parameterized test count decides the contract size. Coverage remains a separate measured gate. The 26→44→46 growth is a reason to audit classifications, not evidence that approved behavior can be deleted.

## 4. Review corrections as deltas, independently

Replace the blanket test-defect routing in §4 with a scoped correction rule. For high-risk work, blind A corrects the affected tests and fresh blind B reviews the changed oracle/tests plus every dependent case. Supply the prior accepted test checkpoint, approved public contract, test finding/diff, and dependency mapping derived from those tests and approved requirements. The coordinator converts implementation-derived discoveries into a public-contract reproduction or returns new meaning to intake. Exclude C's implementation/conversation, implementation-bearing status, and coverage-line maps; copying a D report may breach blindness. Unchanged accepted portions retain their review evidence.

B may accept a revised checkpoint after a delta review only when the retained portions are unaffected and their prior accepted evidence remains applicable. A shared parser, observer, fixture, tolerance, schema, or expectation change expands the review scope. A changed public meaning first needs intake approval; it or an inadequate dependency mapping then requires a fresh complete review of affected behavior. The reviewer may expand scope; the author may not declare their own correction safe. Label tests added after implementation as supplemental; a revised checkpoint does not recreate original test-first evidence.

Amend §9 to count each completed correction review as a round in its recorded window. Preserve previous windows, spending, and C repairs. Keep diagnosis after two nonacceptances. A new session, renamed task, or narrower review does not replenish allowances. Lower-tier corrections retain fresh independent final review whenever that tier requires it.

Clarify that independence requires separation between authors and reviewers, not mandatory loss of an author's own context. Initial high-risk roles remain fresh roots. A may continue its own test-author session within the same approved work while blindness remains intact; C may continue its own implementation session for an authorized repair. Never convert an exposed A/B session into a blind role by clearing context or rerunning clean diagnostics. Use a fresh author when exposure, changed scope, or excessive context warrants it. B/D correction verdicts still use fresh independent review sessions and never inherit implementation conversation. Continuing an author session grants no new editing scope, budget or repair. This continuation exception requires owner approval along with the tier policy.

## 5. Make evidence validity explicit

Expand §9's reuse paragraph and §4's handoff paragraph. Use a compact evidence record: tested revision/tree; test revision; canonical command; dependency-lock identity; relevant environment/tool versions and platform; integration base; exact result and coverage; unresolved limits. One ordinary receipt in ignored artifacts may link command output or discussions; keep final candidate identity/results outside its tracked tree. Fingerprint material ignored/untracked inputs separately because a Git revision does not identify them ([baseline-identity lesson](../../LESSONS/20260930T184757Z-setup-coordinator-ignored-input-identity.md)). Do not build a controller, state store, or evidence database.

Reuse a result only while every relevant input remains unchanged and no new failure challenges it. A changed environment invalidates affected results even if the commit is unchanged. Under current policy, incremental checks guide edits; they do not replace canonical full local verification of the exact submission candidate or required continuous integration (CI) platforms ([§6](../agentic-software-delivery-v1.0/DELIVERY-SYSTEM-SPEC.md#6-ordinary-github-ci)). Retain 100% measured statements and branches across instrumentable owned runtime, globally and per package, including never-imported files and applicable subprocesses. Disclose exclusions and unsupported measurement.

Keep one Current state. Each handoff should contain only decision, revision, classified findings, evidence links, next role, and remaining allowances. A/B receive a public-contract packet separate from implementation-bearing status. Link earlier accepted reports instead of recopying them.

Before expensive dependent checks, use cheap bounded prerequisites: actual interpreter/platform and lock identity, installation/import health, isolated or nested-environment creation where required, and actual reviewer launch/authentication/connectivity when the route requires review. A configured reviewer name is not proof it can review. Stop dependent phases on prerequisite failure; retain applicable independent results and diagnose before retrying. A known-good environment is reusable only for its recorded inputs and current health, not forever because its path is unchanged.

When product/build versions derive from Git, record the version inputs and resolved version with build/install evidence, including relevant commit/tag/dirty state and build configuration. Identical source bytes at a different commit can generate a different artifact/version. PR head, synthetic test-merge and actual merge/squash/rebase identities can differ; never reuse build/install claims solely because source-tree hashes match.

### Optional owner-approved CI-authoritative submission route

Consider a separate policy pilot for settled mechanical or bounded conventional work: focused public-behavior tests and inexpensive local checks before opening/updating a PR; full canonical checks on every required platform plus independent review before an owner-authorized merge. This changes §§4/6/9's local submission gate and known-failure/submission semantics; it is not available under today's rules. SIMrecon installation would need separate approved alignment if adopted. Record the approved mode, eligible scope, required evidence and responsible worker/reviewer in the task. Keep full local verification for high risk, diagnosis, uncertain evidence, and tasks outside the pilot. Initially exclude changes to the alternative's own workflows, test discovery/coverage, protection, build/release proof or authority; a small diff cannot approve its own gate.

Eligibility first requires read-only verification of effective native target-branch protections/rulesets, intended trusted check sources, current-base/integration requirements and applicable bypass settings. The required aggregate must fail for any failed, cancelled, skipped, neutral, absent or incomplete platform/shard/report; a successful aggregate name alone is insufficient. GitHub can accept skipped/neutral required-check conclusions and protection can permit bypasses ([official protection documentation](https://docs.github.com/en/repositories/configuring-branches-and-merges-in-your-repository/managing-protected-branches/about-protected-branches)). A green PR does not prove enforcement. Unknown or ineffective controls block this route; configure them only through separately authorized setup. These native controls still do not enforce role blindness or file ownership.

Full CI must identify the tested head, base/integration state, artifact/version and environment, and cover the complete owned runtime per platform/package. Independent review must assess that final candidate and required evidence. Head, base, dependency, check, environment or generated-version changes invalidate affected claims and require renewed checks/review. If the chosen integration/version strategy cannot supply the required exact artifact/candidate proof before merge, defer eligibility rather than accept tree equivalence. No merge or release authority is delegated by this proposal.

An opened PR awaiting full CI is unverified, never ready. Known applicable failures block submission until classified repair and relevant local checks establish that their cause is addressed; never open a PR solely to run a known failing candidate. Failure first discovered in a pilot PR's full CI stops readiness and routes within existing budgets/allowances. A repair update needs the approved focused local gate, then renewed full CI; preserve failed attempts and rerun invalidated evidence. The proposed exception permits submission with an unexecuted full local suite, not false passing claims, ignored CI failures, or merges before proof. The worker records readiness; the independent reviewer returns a verdict; required native checks and the owner's explicit merge action remain distinct. Default local gating continues if the pilot fails or prerequisites lapse.

### Reduce measured waiting and test hotspots

Use existing host/CI wait or notification facilities for terminal completion, failure or required action. Yield during unchanged status rather than spend model turns repeatedly reading it. Probe actual host capabilities and connectivity first; a disconnected reviewer/wait cannot run indefinitely or imply review completion. Prefer bounded deterministic waits with explicit timeout/failure handling; unsupported host setup is separate authorized work. Do not introduce a scheduler, daemon, approval controller or automatic repair loop.

Profile the project's own slow tests before restructuring. Reuse immutable, digest-identified builds only when all material build/version inputs match. Keep installation environments isolated; consolidate repeated clean-control installs only if distinct independence, clean-state, packaging and negative-control claims remain proved. Do not delete tests merely because they are slow. For CI shards, require every shard, retain failures and combine complete coverage separately for each platform/package, including never-imported owned files and subprocesses. Never union platforms to conceal one platform's missing coverage or accept an incomplete shard set. Any helper/shard change needs its own authorized infrastructure task, independent review and the unchanged full coverage gate.

## 7. Select model quality and reasoning effort by the decision

Treat agent sophistication as the ability to derive a correct oracle, recognize missing semantics, use tools accurately, and challenge a plausible result. Role names and code volume are poor proxies. A mistaken expected value can make every test and implementation pass for the wrong product. Therefore A/B are not default candidates for weaker models than C. Separate model capability, reasoning effort, relevant domain evidence, and context quality; extra effort cannot settle an unapproved convention.

Proposed starting choices, subject to task-specific calibration:

| Role or route | Demanding work | Selection rule |
|---|---|---|
| A | Derive distinguishing fixtures, numerical expectations/tolerances and binary semantics without implementation | For scientific/binary/custom-oracle work, use a configuration demonstrated on that semantic complexity, normally at a supported high reasoning-effort setting. Simpler established expectations may justify a cheaper configuration only after calibration. |
| B | Independently recalculate the oracle; detect shared-helper assumptions, false rejection and weak discrimination | Meet the oracle complexity at least as conservatively as A. Require independent derivation, not endorsement of A's explanation; freshness alone does not remove a shared misconception. |
| C | Build the smallest correct product within frozen tests, interfaces, memory and failure boundaries | Match implementation complexity and tools; use high reasoning effort for numerical, binary, concurrency or resource reasoning. A simple implementation can need less effort than its oracle. |
| D | Audit actual contract behavior, permitted positive inputs, evidence validity and simplicity | Match the hardest acceptance claim and potential defect impact; start high for scientific/binary review. Do not infer a cheap review from green tests/coverage; the positive-input lesson demonstrates the gap. |
| Mechanical worker / policy reviewer | Apply a deterministic diff / resolve rule interactions and authority | Mechanical work may use a cheaper calibrated configuration with deterministic checks. Policy review needs enough reasoning for cross-document contradictions; it is not merely proofreading. |
| Bounded worker + reviewer | Repair settled behavior or maintain approved infrastructure; assess both test and product/gate changes | Calibrate each responsibility separately. Keep fresh independent review; promote to the high-risk route if oracle or permission uncertainty emerges. |

Reuse applicable bounded calibration evidence for an unchanged model/effort and task family; probe unrepresented complexity or changed configurations rather than benchmark every role anew. Use public/synthetic distinguishing cases: legitimate alternative outputs, plausible wrong results, positive and rejection boundaries, unit/profile/endian traps, and known oracle defects. Record correct decisions, false acceptances, missed defects, unnecessary rejection, rework and owner effort alongside cost/latency. Do not expose live implementation or lesson entries to A/B through calibration; use coordinator-prepared, independently reviewed public material. Same-model reviewers are allowed but can share assumptions. A different model is an optional hypothesis to evaluate, not proof of independence or an extra mandatory reviewer.

Choose the route first, then record the per-role configuration, effort and reason in the approved packet/budget. Promote complexity or increase effort after a concrete diagnosis such as an oracle error or an unresolved tool limitation; return missing semantics to intake. A model swap never clears source exposure, resets rounds/repairs, expands permissions, or authorizes another attempt. After two B nonacceptances, preserve §4/§9's diagnosis/stop rule; model escalation alone is no resolution. Charge calibration, failed launches, escalation and rework to the approved budget; obtain an extension when needed. No automatic fallback or retry loop.

Luna/Sol or other family names are illustrative options, not a fixed capability ranking or price claim. Verify actual available models, supported effort settings and applicable pricing at execution if needed. The pilot must compare complete cost per independently accepted task, including reviews, checks, failures and owner time. Passing known cases or a small pilot does not prove quality equivalence or a low defect-escape rate.

## 8. Roll out with measurable acceptance

Adoption order matters:

1. Owner approves tier boundaries/ownership, correction scope, model/effort selection criteria, gate mode and a bounded pilot budget. CI-authoritative eligibility additionally requires the verified native-control/integration prerequisites in §5. Prepare one proposed spec patch; retain Version 1.0 and Git revision history.
2. Independently review the patch. Update the installed/generated AGENTS, TASK template, intake prompt, directly affected role procedures, README and workflow examples together. Check for contradictions. Do not activate only a permissive sentence while retaining incompatible restrictions elsewhere.
3. Pilot one documentation task, one conventional bounded repair, one authorized infrastructure task and one high-risk public/synthetic task. Agree acceptance criteria before starting; do not rerun completed owner-data conversion solely for a benchmark. Keep in-flight tasks on their original approved rules unless the owner explicitly approves migration. Migration cannot erase earlier attempts or reset spent budgets/allowances. Suspend the affected lighter route if a pilot exposes a missed material obligation, source exposure, false acceptance or unresolved gap; classify/resolve it within existing allowances before retrying. Keep required independent review and gates throughout.
4. Compare results, then approve wider adoption or revise the proposal. If initial cases pass, an explicitly budgeted follow-up of ten comparable maintenance tasks could test repeatability; ten is a proposed sample, not proof of defect-rate equivalence. Keep changes to route, model and verification placement distinguishable. Do not merge/release automatically.

Acceptance checks: correct tier recorded before execution; unchanged approved behavior preserved; independent reviewers used where required; a shared-oracle correction expands review scope; invalidated evidence reruns; full exact-candidate verification and coverage pass under the explicitly approved gate mode; no concealed failure, scope expansion, self-approval, or multiplied allowance; one concise status and usable handoff.

Measure elapsed critical path from task/PR start to readiness/merge separately from aggregate resource cost. Overlapping review, tests and CI durations are not additive elapsed time; all model usage and runner minutes still count toward cost. Record timestamps/dependencies, time to PR, time to merge, repeated checks and owner waits without double-counting overlap. Amend §9 metrics to record model/effort identity, per-role model responses, input/cached/output tokens, available monetary cost, elapsed and owner-wait minutes, check wall time, scenarios versus coverage examples, review windows/rounds, repairs, found/missed defects and source exposure. Missing metering is “unknown,” not zero. Avoid double-counting cumulative counters or child sessions. Compare like risk tiers, semantic complexity and task sizes; vary model/effort and route separately where feasible so attribution is interpretable. Report totals and role attribution together, including calibration/rework and owner effort. Prices require current verified evidence and complete usage; cached-input percentage alone is not a savings percentage. Set savings targets after a complete baseline, and report the small sample's limits.

Owner decisions remain: accept the middle tier's loss of blind test construction and its explicit infrastructure ownership; approve tier boundaries/promotion triggers and the policy-review path; accept complete-request authorization for lower tiers; approve scoped correction review and author-session continuation; select model/effort calibration and escalation criteria; choose gate mode, pilot budget and acceptable overhead/defect criteria. CI-authoritative submission needs its own approved policy change and verified native prerequisites; monitor/hotspot tooling remains separately scoped. Recommendation: keep root `LESSONS/`; relocation would need its own approved migration. This document authorizes none of those changes.
